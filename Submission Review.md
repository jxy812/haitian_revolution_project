## Individual reflection starting draft

Haiti’s revolution threatened more than French control of one colony. It challenged a system in which powerful countries built wealth by treating Black people as property. If formerly enslaved people could defeat a European army and govern themselves, slaveholders elsewhere could no longer claim that slavery was inevitable. Withholding recognition and demanding compensation helped protect that unequal order. Calling Haiti a failure also shifted attention away from the pressures imposed on the new country. However, “the rest of the world” was not united: some abolitionists and Black communities saw Haiti as an example of liberation. Haiti’s later problems should be studied honestly, including internal conflicts, without making them evidence that emancipation failed. The revolution succeeded in destroying slavery and colonial rule; the world’s response made sustaining that freedom more difficult.

## Research corrections and limits

- The 1801 constitution retained a formal French connection; it was not the declaration of independence.
- The 1825 ordinance belongs to the aftermath, not the independence war.
- The 1838 figure is a revised total of 90 million francs, not 90 million added to the original demand.
- The original indemnity, loans used to pay it, and later refinanced debts are different obligations. Sources’ 1878, 1921–22, and 1947 dates describe different scopes.
- The roughly $560 million estimate values historical payments; the $20-billion-plus estimate models foregone growth. Neither is a verified current legal award or a complete explanation of Haiti’s difficulties.
- The original organizer’s “Georges Banton” should read Georges Danton. The site avoids assigning the 1794 abolition vote to one politician.
- The Haiti Reader link was a front-matter preview. Ghachem’s chapter could be read only as an abstract. Neither is counted as substantive supporting evidence.
- The AP article originally listed only by title has now been located and cited alongside the UN report for the April 2025 commission announcement. Neither is presented as evidence of the commission’s current results.
- Natural Earth provides generalized coastlines and modern boundaries; the map clearly distinguishes these from changing historical control. No invented archival map or AI-generated historical image is presented as evidence.
