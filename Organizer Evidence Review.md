## Claims handled carefully

- **“The amount exceeded actual losses.”** UN News makes this claim, but the organizer does not define the property valuation or supply a calculation. The site does not turn it into an independently verified numerical finding. Its fairness argument rests on the documented military pressure, compensation for colonists, and burden placed on the formerly enslaved society.
- **“France offered to lend money.”** Revised to distinguish France’s demand from lending by banks and bondholders. Treating the government and lenders as one actor obscures how the debt worked.
- **“Does that truly count as freedom?”** Developed into an argument rather than answered with an absolute no. Haiti’s political independence was real, while its economic choices remained constrained.
- **Not every source link is usable evidence.** The Haiti Reader file was a front-matter preview; the Ghachem chapter could be reviewed only as an abstract. Neither is counted as if its full historical argument had been read. Eddins is used substantively; the AP item is now included.

## What the deeper analysis adds

1. **A cause-and-effect explanation:** competing demands weakened colonial agreement and pushed the conflict beyond reforms for people already free.
2. **A primary-source argument:** the 1801 constitution simultaneously abolished slavery, preserved a French connection, restricted cultivators, and gave Louverture life tenure. These provisions show why different forms of freedom could conflict.
3. **A reason for Boyer’s decision:** recognition and security could make acceptance seem practical under pressure. A pragmatic decision does not make the terms fair.
4. **A financial mechanism:** borrowing, charges, repayment, and lost investment connect the indemnity to public resources without claiming it explains every modern crisis.
5. **A clear reparations judgment:** restitution is justified by the particular coerced extraction, while amount, administration, and Haitian participation still require serious consideration.
6. **A fair assessment of success:** ending slavery and French rule succeeded; democratic power and economic independence remained contested. Later hardship does not erase the revolution’s achievements.
